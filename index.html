About SDG 6

SDG 6 aims to ensure access to water and sanitation for all. Despite progress, billions of people still lack these basic services. Water scarcity affects more than 40% of the global population and is projected to rise.

Frequently Asked Questions

SDG 6's full title is "Ensure availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all," covering drinking water, sanitation, hygiene, wastewater, water-use efficiency, ecosystem protection, and cooperation. It has eight targets and related indicators that track progress on access, quality, efficiency, governance, and international support.

SDG 6

The global monitoring definition of a "safely managed drinking water service" is drinking water from an improved source that is located on the household premises, available when needed, and free from fecal and priority chemical contamination. This is tracked as SDG indicator 6.1.1, the proportion of the population using safely managed drinking water services.

Sanitation is emphasized because safe toilets and waste management reduce diarrheal and other infectious diseases, protecting public health at scale. Improved sanitation also helps safeguard groundwater and surface water from contamination and supports dignity, gender equality, and safety, especially for women and girls.

Many SDG‑aligned water initiatives work by constructing or rehabilitating community systems such as wells, piped supplies, or small networks, combined with governance and cost‑recovery arrangements. Long‑term functionality depends on local operation and maintenance capacity, so projects often include training community water committees or local technicians.

Depending on geography and hydrogeology, projects may use deep or bore wells, gravity‑fed or pumped piped networks, rainwater harvesting systems, point‑of‑use filtration, and decentralized wastewater or nature‑based systems like constructed wetlands. SDG 6 also explicitly encourages technologies for water efficiency, wastewater treatment, recycling, and safe reuse.

Organizations working on SDG 6 typically offer roles in in‑country field programs, remote research or data support, and advocacy or awareness campaigns. Interested volunteers are usually asked to apply through an expression‑of‑interest form or contact channel where skills and availability can be matched to current projects.

Local participation is a formal target under SDG 6 (Target 6.B), which calls for supporting and strengthening community involvement in water and sanitation management. Effective programs place community members in leadership roles for planning, tariff setting, routine maintenance, and system oversight so that services remain sustainable.

Water‑and‑sanitation projects under SDG 6 operate across all regions, with particularly intense activity in Asia, sub‑Saharan Africa, and Latin America where access gaps remain largest. Many international NGOs report operations in dozens of countries simultaneously, often focusing on rural areas and informal settlements.

Individuals can support SDG 6 by donating to credible WASH (water, sanitation and hygiene) organizations, volunteering skills, partnering through research or business, or amplifying evidence‑based advocacy. Everyday actions such as conserving water, supporting equitable tariffs, and engaging in local water‑governance forums also contribute to Goal 6 targets.

SDG 6 includes a specific target to "expand international cooperation and capacity‑building support" for water and sanitation, which inherently involves government engagement. National and local governments set policies, standards, and financing frameworks, while collaborations with civil society and the private sector help deliver and maintain services on the ground.
